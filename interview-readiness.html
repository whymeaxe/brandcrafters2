Truth-based mock interview preparation

Prepare the applicant—not a memorised performance.

Our interview-readiness service trains applicants to understand questions, select relevant facts, answer concisely and remain consistent with the submitted application under pressure.

See the TRUST Protocol
Our preparation rule

Natural clarity is more credible than perfect-sounding memorisation.

We identify the applicant’s real facts, likely decision topics and communication patterns. Mock interviews are used to improve judgement—not to teach false answers.

EOE TRUST Answer Protocol™

A simple structure for answering under pressure.

Truth

Use accurate facts. Do not hide, invent or exaggerate.

Relevance

Answer the question asked—not the speech you prepared.

Understanding

Listen fully and seek clarification when genuinely needed.

Structure

Give the key answer first, then only necessary context.

Tone

Remain calm, respectful, natural and consistent.

What happens in preparation

We train decision-ready communication in stages.

1

Application briefing

The applicant reviews every material fact submitted in the DS-160 and supporting case record.

2

Topic mapping

Likely questions are organised around purpose, funding, ties, history, course or business logic.

3

Baseline mock interview

We observe listening, relevance, accuracy, brevity, tone and pressure behaviour.

4

Targeted correction

We identify vague, contradictory, overlong, defensive or rehearsed responses.

5

Pressure consistency

Follow-up questions test whether the applicant truly understands the case.

6

Final readiness review

We confirm the applicant can explain the real case without relying on a script.

Communication risks we address
  • Answering a different question
  • Giving unnecessary detail
  • Sounding memorised or coached
  • Contradicting the DS-160
  • Using vague course or trip reasons
  • Not understanding sponsor finances
  • Becoming defensive after a follow-up
  • Changing facts under pressure
  • Speaking for another family applicant

Interview preparation cannot repair a false application.
If the DS-160 is materially inaccurate or the case is not ready, we may recommend correcting the application strategy or seeking legal advice before further mock interviews.
Readiness outcome

Confidence should come from understanding the case.

At the end of preparation, the applicant should know the submitted facts, understand the purpose and funding, recognise areas requiring precise explanation, and answer without depending on memorised sentences.
